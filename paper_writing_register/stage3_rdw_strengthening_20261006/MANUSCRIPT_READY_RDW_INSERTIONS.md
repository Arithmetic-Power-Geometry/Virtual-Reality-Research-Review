# Stage 3 Manuscript-Ready RDW Strengthening

## Methods insertion — geometry and outcomes
The controlled RDW benchmark used four declared physical geometries while holding the virtual environment, controller implementations, seed pairing, reset policy, and principal outcome definitions fixed. G01 was an open 10 x 10 m square (100 m2); G02 added one 5 m internal vertical segment to the same boundary; G03 added two 5 m internal segments; and G04 used an asymmetric 12 x 8 m rectangle (96 m2) with one 4 m internal segment. Thus the benchmark moves from an open convex space to progressively occluded and asymmetric tracked spaces without changing the basic simulation semantics.

Outcomes were analyzed in two parts. Completion was treated as a paired binary outcome over the 100 common seeds per geometry. Reset burden was defined as resets per 100 m and compared only on seeds for which both controllers in the pair completed; failed runs were not assigned artificial reset scores. Exact paired completion tests were reported separately from paired reset-burden differences. For reset burden, the analysis reports the mean paired difference, deterministic bootstrap 95% interval, Wilcoxon signed-rank inference with BH and Holm multiplicity adjustment, and standardized paired effect size d_z.

## Results insertion — effect magnitude and regime reversal
The strongest standardized reset-burden effects occurred in G01: Vis-Poly minus S2C had d_z=1.403, Vis-Poly minus P2R d_z=0.773, and S2C minus P2R d_z=-0.977. Completion also favored S2C and P2R over Vis-Poly by eight percentage points in G01 (exact p=0.007812 for both comparisons). In G03, however, the direction changed. Vis-Poly minus S2C was -1.004 resets/100 m (95% bootstrap CI -1.588 to -0.417; Holm p=0.00945; d_z=-0.376), and Vis-Poly completed 13 percentage points more seeds than P2R (exact p=0.010622). Across G01-G04, every controller pair exhibited at least one sign reversal in its paired reset-burden contrast.

Within each geometry, a Friedman test on triple-complete seeds also detected controller differences: G01 chi2=100.778 (n=92, p=1.31e-22), G02 chi2=14.683 (n=96, p=6.48e-4), G03 chi2=6.059 (n=62, p=0.0483), and G04 chi2=9.531 (n=94, p=0.00852). These tests establish within-geometry heterogeneity but are not presented as a factorial interaction test. Because the preserved manuscript-level summaries do not encode the full cross-geometry seed covariance needed for a mixed interaction model, the controller-by-geometry result is expressed as an auditable regime-reversal result rather than an invented interaction p-value.

## Discussion insertion — bounded interpretation
The RDW experiment does not support a universal controller leaderboard. Its stronger result is structural: controller ordering depends on the physical geometry and on whether performance is judged by completion or by reset burden conditional on completion. G01 strongly favors S2C in conditional reset burden, whereas G03 reverses the Vis-Poly/S2C ordering. Vis-Poly also changes from an eight-point completion disadvantage relative to P2R in G01 to a 13-point advantage in G03. Such reversals are precisely the type of regime dependence that a geometry-agnostic aggregate ranking would conceal.

The computational-cost audit adds a second trade-off dimension. On the common CI runner, median controller-decision cost was 251.448 microseconds for Vis-Poly, 2.022 for S2C, and 3.938 for P2R. These values are implementation- and runner-specific microbenchmarks, not end-to-end frame-time measurements. They nevertheless show that the geometrically richer visibility-polygon controller carries substantially greater decision overhead in the tested implementation.

No live-user validation is included. Accordingly, the benchmark is not used to infer gain detectability, cybersickness, presence, comfort, preference, or usability. Its role is computational: it demonstrates that even under common simulation semantics, completion, reset burden, geometry, and controller cost form a multi-dimensional comparison in which a single universal ranking is not supported.
