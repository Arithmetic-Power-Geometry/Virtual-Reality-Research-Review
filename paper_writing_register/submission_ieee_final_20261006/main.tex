\begin{abstract}
Virtual-reality (VR) research couples hardware, sensing, rendering, interaction, locomotion, perception, networking, and human factors, so results that share a label may still answer different questions. We organize a verified evidence library of 174 records into 173 canonical reports; 171 complete reports were retrieved and assessed, yielding 165 direct eligible VR/XR reports and six supporting/context reports, with two reports not retrieved. We introduce a semantic-comparability framework and a six-level reproducibility taxonomy, then test the framework through a controlled redirected-walking (RDW) benchmark implemented as reproducible software and executed through versioned workflows. Among eight pre-specified contradiction families, seven were comparability-limited, while one was a genuine controlled regime effect. In 1,200 planned controller-condition cells across four geometries, Visibility-Polygon, Steer-to-Center, and Potential-to-Redirect completed 384/400, 378/400, and 381/400 runs, respectively. Paired inference showed geometry-dependent rank changes rather than a universal winner. Median controller-decision costs were 251.448, 2.022, and 3.938 $\mu$s on the common runner. The results argue for regime-aware comparison, explicit evidence obligations, failure-aware outcomes, and provenance-preserving computational evaluation in VR research.
\end{abstract}
